## Hard constraints (official, Veo 3.1 GA — `veo-3.1-generate-001`)
- **Duration: 4, 6, or 8 seconds ONLY.** (No 5, no 10. 24 FPS.) Pick from {4,6,8}.
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 or 9:16.
- **Resolution:** 720p or 1080p (4k is preview-models only).
- **Prompt length cap:** ~1,024 tokens (~750 words). Detailed is fine; this is the ceiling.
- **Native audio** is generated by default — you MUST direct it or the model hallucinates
  ambient sound/laughter. For a silent use (e.g. frame-scrub extraction) disable audio if the
  backend exposes it, or at minimum specify the intended ambient bed.
- **Capabilities:** text-to-video, image-to-video (single start frame), **first-and-last-frame**
  (provide start AND end image → Veo interpolates the transition, with audio), reference/
  "ingredients" images (up to 3), scene extension, add/remove object.

## Minimum structure checklist (prompt-quality gate)
Before submitting, confirm the prompt hits all four — a prompt missing any of these is not ready:
- [ ] **Shot** — composition/framing named (wide, close-up, medium, two-shot, etc.)
- [ ] **Camera** — camera work/movement named (dolly, crane, pan, static, POV, etc.) or explicitly static
- [ ] **Motion** — the subject's action described concretely (not just a pose)
- [ ] **Duration** — the requested duration is one of {4, 6, 8}s and matches what was asked/confirmed

## The official five-part formula
```
[Cinematography] + [Subject] + [Action] + [Context] + [Style & Ambiance]
```
- **Cinematography** — camera work + shot composition (the most powerful tone lever).
- **Subject** — the main character/focal point.
- **Action** — what the subject is doing.
- **Context** — environment + background detail.
- **Style & ambiance** — overall aesthetic, mood, lighting.

### Calibration: how long is a *good* Veo prompt?
Google's own canonical examples are **one dense paragraph, ~40–80 words** — NOT a multi-section
wall of text. Detail comes from precise, concrete nouns/verbs, not length. The structured/labeled
approach (CAMERA: / MOTION: / LIGHTING: / NEGATIVE:) from `cinematic-motion-language` is a valid,
higher-control variant and Veo 3.1 ("stronger prompt adherence") handles it — but do not mistake
*more words* for *better*. Tight + concrete + formula-complete beats long + vague.

## Official example prompts (verbatim — the gold standard for length/detail)

**Five-part formula example:**
> Medium shot, a tired corporate worker, rubbing his temples in exhaustion, in front of a bulky
> 1980s computer in a cluttered office late at night. The scene is lit by the harsh fluorescent
> overhead lights and the green glow of the monochrome monitor. Retro aesthetic, shot as if on
> 1980s color film, slightly grainy.

**Crane shot (camera movement):**
> Crane shot starting low on a lone hiker and ascending high above, revealing they are standing on
> the edge of a colossal, mist-filled canyon at sunrise, epic fantasy style, awe-inspiring, soft
> morning light.

**Shallow depth of field (lens/optical):**
> Close-up with very shallow depth of field, a young woman's face, looking out a bus window at the
> passing city lights with her reflection faintly visible on the glass, inside a bus at night
> during a rainstorm, melancholic mood with cool blue tones, moody, cinematic.

**DeepMind world-building (short prompt, rich scene):**
> A snow-covered plain of iridescent moon-dust under twilight skies. Thirty-foot crystalline
> flowers bloom, refracting light into slow-moving rainbows. A fur-cloaked figure walks between
> these colossal blossoms, leaving the only footprints in untouched dust.

## Essential techniques
- **Camera movement vocabulary:** dolly shot, tracking shot, crane shot, aerial view, slow pan,
  POV shot. **Composition:** wide / close-up / extreme close-up / low angle / two-shot.
  **Lens & focus:** shallow depth of field, wide-angle, soft focus, macro, deep focus.
- **Directing the soundstage:** `Dialogue:` use quotation marks ("We have to leave now.");
  `SFX:` describe clearly (SFX: thunder cracks in the distance); `Ambient noise:` define the bed.
- **Negative prompts — describe the world you WANT, not "no X".** Prefer "a desolate landscape
  with no buildings or roads" over "no man-made structures". (Veo's negativePrompt field exists,
  but positive description of absence is stronger.)
- **Timestamp prompting** (sequence control within a clip): `[00:00-00:02] ...` `[00:02-00:04] ...`
  Each segment gets its own shot + action + SFX. Good for a clip that must hit beats in order.

## First-and-last-frame workflow (Veo 3.1's standout control — use for hard transitions)
For a transition you need to *land precisely* (e.g. day→night, A-pose→B-pose, POV swap), don't
hope a text instruction darkens the sky — **generate the end frame explicitly and let Veo
interpolate**:
1. Start frame = your source still (or a Nano Banana / imgen gen).
2. End frame = generate the target state (e.g. the same scene at night) with an image model.
3. Veo prompt describes the *transition + motion* between them. Official example:
   > The camera performs a smooth 180-degree arc shot, starting with the front-facing view of the
   > singer and circling around her to seamlessly end on the POV shot from behind her on stage.

This yields far more reliable transitions than single-start-frame + "the scene becomes night".

## Community / advanced notes (snubroot, community-verified)
- **Dialogue without subtitles:** use the colon format — `She says: "line"` (colon suppresses
  on-screen text); or add `(no subtitles)`.
- **Audio hallucination fix:** always state the expected background audio, else Veo invents it
  (e.g. unwanted "live studio audience" laughter on a joke).
- **8-second dialogue rule:** ~1 sentence of speech per 8s; longer rushes, single words gibberish.
- **Movement quality keywords** + explicit camera-position notes give natural, controlled motion.
