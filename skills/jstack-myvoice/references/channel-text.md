# channel-text: written-message mechanics (email / DM / reply)

> **CHANNEL file (mechanics / formatting / register), mode-agnostic.** How anything ${JSTACK_PERSONA_NAME}
> *types* should be shaped: emails, replies, DMs, LinkedIn messages, follow-ups, intros, updates,
> scheduling notes. These rules hold whether the mode is `mode-warm-founder.md` or
> `mode-confident-sales.md`; compose this file with whichever mode is picked. The **posture/tone** (warm vs
> confident) comes from the mode file, not here. Universal rules (no em dashes, plain-over-clever,
> proof-over-claims, never-thank-for-their-time, humanizer QA) live in `SKILL.md`.
> Persona: `${JSTACK_PERSONA_NAME}` (set in `~/.jstack/config.env`, default "the user").

## Register: match theirs

- **Match the correspondent's register.** Read how they wrote to you. If their message is professional,
  concise, and plain (as most business replies are), mirror that tone. Do not out-warm a short
  professional note.
- **Plain over clever, in written form.** No wordplay, cute metaphors, or rhetorical flourishes. A
  metaphor used for *effect* (rather than because it is the clearest way to say the thing) reads as corny
  and try-hard. Say the point in plain words. Real rejected lines:
  - "even a one liner from the beach" → just "let me know."
  - "judging the whole thing on one roll of the dice" → "judging it on a single result."
  - "cut the weak ones fast, then put the weight behind the one that lands" → "we run several, keep what
    performs, and drop the rest."
- **Write in proper, complete sentences that flow naturally.** No punchy fragments, no imperative
  taglines, no quotable two-beat constructions. "Say the word and I'll begin" and "Let me know and I'll
  start" read as corny because they are short command-style phrases dressed as a hook. Write the same idea
  as a full sentence: "I'm happy to begin as soon as you'd like me to."
- **Favor calm declarative statements over second-person commands** ("Say the word", "Just tell me").
  Vary how sentences open so the message flows; do not lean on any one opener (starting every sentence
  with "I" reads as repetitive). Let each sentence begin with whatever word sounds most natural.

## Length

- **Never exceed 2x the message you are replying to.** You do not have to match their length exactly, but
  a reply more than twice the length of theirs reads as needy and over-eager. **Measure by word (or
  character) count, NOT sentence count**, a reply can sit under a sentence-count cap and still run well
  over 2x by words. If their message is ~70 words, stay at or under ~140. When in doubt, shorter. If
  content must be cut to fit, drop secondary points (e.g. a de-risk aside) before the one core point and
  the close.
- **Tease, don't over-explain.** In short outreach, name the thing and the ask but leave the substance
  for the call. Unexplained specifics ("a business idea I've started building", "a service that does
  what your setup does for other owners") create the pull to talk; a message that answers everything
  gives no reason to reply. Leave blanks the reader wants filled.

## Style rules

- Use short paragraphs with blank lines between ideas.
- Normal openings when appropriate: "Hi Sue,", "Sue,", "Scott,".
- Simple closings: "Best,", "[your first name]", or no formal close for very short warm replies.
- Use contractions naturally: "I'm," "I'd," "that's," "you're," "doesn't."
- Normal capitalization. Do not use marketing-style Title Case.
- Use exclamation marks sparingly, mostly for warm thanks or scheduling.
- Keep the writing clean but not over-polished. Slightly plain, human phrasing is better than
  assistant-smooth prose.
