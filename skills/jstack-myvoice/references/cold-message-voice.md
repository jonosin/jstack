# cold-message-voice: cold DM / email / InMail voice

> The voice file for **cold** first-touch outreach to strangers: cold DMs, cold emails, InMails,
> connection-request notes, cold openers and subject lines. Persona is `${JSTACK_PERSONA_NAME}` (set
> in `~/.jstack/config.env`).
>
> **Division of labour:** this file owns the cold *voice* (how it should sound). `jstack-coldmsg` owns
> the cold *strategy* (the reply psychology, the quantified levers, opener shapes with measured lift,
> personalization tiers, cadence, the metrics scoreboard, and the worked second-brain-agency example).
> When drafting a real cold batch, work the structure with `jstack-coldmsg`
> (`~/jstack/skills/jstack-coldmsg/references/framework.md` is the deep playbook) and render the voice
> here. Do not duplicate the framework; cross-reference it.

## The reflex you're writing against
The keep/delete decision fires in ~0.3-0.5s by pattern-match, **before** your copy is read. A cold
message only clears the reflex when it carries **one specific, checkable detail about that exact
prospect** that could not have been mass-produced. The fastest delete trigger is a confident claim
that's equally true of every competitor in their vertical. Everything below serves that one specific.

## How a ${JSTACK_PERSONA_NAME} cold message sounds
- **One real, checkable specific, pinned to them.** A number (years, locations, headcount, #events), a
  named product/service/system, a niche, a recent move. Acid test: if the sentence would read true for
  any competitor, rewrite it. No specific found yet = not ready to write the message.
- **Never open with what you do, never pitch in line one.** Open around their specific and end on a
  question they actually want to answer.
- **Plain, slightly informal, human register.** A touch of deliberate imperfection reads as a real
  person; over-polish is itself a template fingerprint that sophisticated buyers screen out. No
  marketing Title Case, no campaign language.
- **Lead with meaning, not the company name.** The brand has no power to a stranger; describe the work
  plainly and let the name surface in the link or signature.
- **Don't leak how you screened them.** Lines like "most companies I look at aren't doing X" expose
  your targeting machinery and make it feel like a swept list. Show you looked at *them*, not at your
  filter.
- **Proof over claims.** A verifiable specific (a real number, a clickable artifact, a quoted review
  line) beats any adjective. Give a tiny finding or artifact free when you can; it removes the
  "believe me" step.
- **No diagnosis frame for someone who doesn't feel the pain.** Telling a stranger their know-how is
  "stuck in heads, is it queryable?" is a verdict on their competence. To someone who lives the pain
  it lands; to someone who doesn't it reads as an insult (the Paul block, `jstack-coldmsg` framework
  §9). When in doubt, switch from a *diagnosis* frame to an *offer* frame ("I clean it up / I do the
  work") and state the pain as a peer observation, not an accusation.

## CTA
- **Tiny and low-friction.** A yes/no or "worth a quick look?" beats "book a 30-minute call." Ask for
  the *reply*, not the meeting.
- **Cheap for you to honor.** Think through the cost of a "yes" before you write the ask. "Want me to
  send a sample over?" silently commits you to expensive free work. Prefer a paid pilot framed as a
  calibration, a short call, or a concrete read.

## Punctuation and finish
- **No em dashes, ever.** Split into two sentences or use a comma/colon/parentheses.
- Keep first touch lean (under ~150 words; far less is better). No "what we do" preamble.

## QA gate before sending (all must pass)
- **Single-person test:** could this exact message have gone to 1,000 people unchanged? If yes, fail.
- **Specificity:** it contains one real, checkable detail about *them*.
- **Length:** lean; no preamble.
- **Voice:** sounds natural and human; no em dashes.
- **Payoff:** the body delivers on whatever the opener implied. An opened curiosity gap that resolves
  to a generic pitch is worse than no gap.

When a cold message is screened on felt pain and built on a real specific, the voice above carries it.
For everything about *which* shape, *which* tier, cadence, and channel mechanics, go to `jstack-coldmsg`.
