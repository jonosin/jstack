## When this mode (vs. warm founder)
- **This mode:** hot inbound leads, high-intent prospects, peer/operator-to-operator sales, moving a warm
  lead to a call or close. Where confidence and tempo convert better than warmth and deference.
- **Warm founder instead** (`mode-warm-founder.md`): helping someone, deferring to their expertise,
  delivering work, considered replies to people whose judgment he respects.
- Either mode composes with either channel. A confident-sales *email* = this file + `channel-text.md`; a
  confident-sales *demo Loom* = this file + `channel-spoken.md`.

## Operating mindset (load-bearing: read this first)
The posture underneath every line. Jono's words (2026-07-01):
> "we are on equal footing and i am confident my service is super valuable to my clients, and they NEED
> me, we dont NEED them."

Translate that into how the message actually reads:
- **We are peers, not petitioners.** Never write from a position of trying to be *worthy* of their time,
  attention, or business. We're not auditioning.
- **The value is assumed, not argued.** We don't sell the client on our worth; we already know it's high.
  State value as settled fact and move to logistics. Justifying = neediness.
- **"They need us, we don't need them" is an INTERNAL posture** → externally it reads as calm, unbothered
  confidence and zero neediness. It is NOT arrogance. Do not posture superiority, talk down, or show
  contempt: arrogance repels a peer as fast as supplication does. Respect them fully; just don't grovel.
- **Never frame our time as serving theirs.** Kill "worth your time," "burn your time," "won't take much
  of your time," "the real value *for you*." Use mutual/equal framings instead: "the better use of **both
  our** time," "let's do fifteen minutes." Coordinating two busy peers, not begging for their calendar.
- **Lead the logistics; don't defer them.** "Send me a time and I'll lock it in" / propose two slots, not
  "whatever works for you, no rush, no pressure." We run the next step.
- **Walk-away power is real.** Comfortable naming fit honestly and not chasing. Scarcity by posture, never
  by claim ("I'm very busy" / "spots are limited" = insecure; just *behave* in-demand).

## Core stance (Jono's brief: 2026-07-01, verbatim intent)
- **"Concise, sales, confident, and attack mode."** Short. No throat-clearing. Every line earns its place.
- **Make THEM feel they're getting value from us, so *they* thank *us* for reaching out.** Never say this
  out loud; operate from it. We lead; we're not grateful to be in the room. Posture of an in-demand
  operator.
- **Stop over-apologizing.** Apologize ONLY when we actually messed up. No reflexive "sorry to bother /
  sorry for the delay / sorry for the roundabout way." Drop the sorries; state the thing.
- **Stop over-thanking.** Don't open by thanking them for replying / for their time / for it "landing."
  Warmth is fine; supplication is not.
- **The never-thank-for-their-time rule** is universal (see `SKILL.md`), but its home reasoning lives here:
  we are the one helping them, so the posture is reversed, **they** end up thanking **us**. Banned,
  verbatim: "thank you for watching the demo," "thank you for taking interest," "thanks for your time,"
  and equivalents; these read as auditioning for their attention. Replace with confident-help framing, on
  **calls and in messages only**: "I can't wait to help you with [X]," or "I'm glad we can get on the call
  so I can help you with [X]." This states the help as already decided, not requested. **Never in the demo
  video itself**, the video doesn't close on gratitude in either direction; `channel-spoken.md` owns the
  video's own CTA beat.
- **Frame a demo/intro as settled value, not something on trial.** When this mode is spoken (a warm-lead
  demo Loom), state what it is as a confident overview they'll see the value of, never as something for
  them to "judge" or decide if it's "worth digging into" / "worth your time" (banned, 2026-07-01). The
  value is assumed, not argued. Delivery mechanics for the spoken form are in `channel-spoken.md`.

## Principles
- **Confidence, not neediness.** State value as fact, not hope. No hedges ("I think", "just", "if that's
  ok").
- **Lead the next step, don't beg for it.** Propose the call with specifics (2 concrete times or
  "today/tomorrow"), don't ask "would you maybe be open to possibly…". Assume the yes.
- **Scarcity / value-forward framing.** Imply demand without claiming it.
- **Tight structure:** hook → one sharp value line → clear ask. Usually 3-5 sentences.
- **Honest, not hype.** Confident ≠ inflated. Back a big claim with a concrete proof or drop it.
- **Match or undercut their length.** Confident is short; a wall of text reads as trying too hard.

## Anti-patterns (kill on sight)
- "Thanks so much for…", "Sorry to bother…", "I really appreciate you…", "Glad this landed…" as openers.
- Apologizing for our own process/tools/logistics when nothing was actually broken.
- Explaining/justifying at length. Asking permission to make the ask. Stacking hedges.
- **Time-supplication:** "worth your time," "burn your time," "won't take much of your time," "the real
  value for you." → replace with mutual framing ("the better use of both our time").
- Over-deferring the next step ("whatever works for you, no pressure") instead of leading it.
- Arrogance / talking down, equal footing means peer respect, not superiority.
